Transportation Rideshare Chicago

Rideshare Demand Forecasting

Where will people need rides next?

I explored whether recent pickup activity and information about different parts of Chicago could help forecast rideshare demand up to two hours into the future.

A citywide view of changing rideshare demand.

01 · Why it matters

Demand moves around a city throughout the day.

A busy area now may be quiet an hour later. Knowing where demand is likely to move gives rideshare operators more time to position vehicles and can reduce unnecessary waiting and idling.

02 · How it worked

Instead of treating Chicago as one number, I looked at the city area by area.

Each census tract represented one part of Chicago. The project used recent pickup activity together with a small amount of context about how those areas relate to one another.

774 Chicago census tracts were included in the study.

03 · The data

Real rideshare trips became a timeline of pickup activity across Chicago.

Trip records came from the Chicago Data Portal and were grouped by area and 15-minute interval. Public Census data provided the additional information describing different parts of the city.

04 · What I found

Adding more context about the city helped the longer-range forecasts hold up better.

I compared several versions of the forecasting approach on a separate period of Chicago rideshare data. The strongest version performed best at each future checkpoint in the two-hour forecast.

8 / 8 future checkpoints

The strongest approach had the lowest prediction error at every checkpoint.

The most noticeable benefit was farther into the future, where several comparison methods became less reliable more quickly.

05 · Looking back

The main lesson was simple: places in a city do not behave independently.

Looking beyond one location's recent history gave the forecasting system a better picture of what was happening across Chicago. If I continued the project, I would focus on extending the dataset and testing the idea at larger scales.
